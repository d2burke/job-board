## 1. Existing Competitors

### A. On-Demand Showing & Agent Assistance Platforms

#### Showami (showami.com)
- **What it does:** Connects licensed real estate agents with other licensed agents to assist with buyer showings on demand. Also supports open house hosting, property photography, rental tours, and inspector/appraiser access.
- **Founded:** 2016
- **Scale:** 80,000+ agents on the platform, thousands of showings per month
- **Pricing:** Pay-per-showing, $45-$400 per showing (set by initiating agent). Showing agents earn $28-$367 per showing. Free to sign up, zero monthly fees.
- **Key stats:** 97% of showings get picked up; typical pickup time under 3 minutes; 4.8-star average review
- **Strength:** Established market leader in the on-demand showing niche with strong network effects
- **Limitation:** Primarily focused on showings; limited scope for broader task marketplace

#### Sticka (stickalocal.com)
- **What it does:** The first all-freelance real estate marketplace. A video-first, peer-to-peer platform where real estate professionals (TCs, agents, loan officers, inspectors, designers, stagers, property managers) can offer services and get booked locally or remotely.
- **Status:** Live beta
- **Model:** No salary, hourly pay, or guaranteed assignments. Professionals create/offer their own services and earn when booked.
- **Differentiator:** Video-first approach (short video posts showcasing services); positions itself as "TikTok meets marketplace" for real estate
- **Limitation:** Very early stage, unproven at scale, no established network effects yet

### B. Transaction Coordination Platforms

#### Transactly (transactly.com)
- **What it does:** Tech-enabled transaction coordination platform. TCs handle up to 90% of all closing tasks.
- **Positioning:** Bills itself as the #1 Real Estate Transaction Coordination Platform
- **Model:** Provides licensed transaction specialists who manage deadlines, paperwork, and compliance

#### Bullpen (bullpenre.com)
- **What it does:** Connects commercial real estate firms with freelance talent for transaction management, site visits, document management, marketing, and deal support.
- **Focus:** Commercial real estate (not residential)
- **Model:** On-demand freelance experts in specific CRE roles

### C. Virtual Assistant Companies

#### REVA Global
- **Founded:** 2013
- **Scale:** 500+ VAs (Philippines-based), 56% with 2+ years tenure
- **Pricing:** ~$10/hour
- **Specialty:** Real estate-exclusive (cold calling, admin, marketing, TC)

#### MyOutDesk
- **Founded:** 2008 (longest-running in the space)
- **Pricing:** ~$10/hour
- **Specialty:** Lead management, CRM integration for real estate agents

#### Virtudesk
- **Founded:** 2016
- **Pricing:** ~$9.55/hour
- **Specialty:** Social media management, multi-channel communication
- **Notable:** Proprietary VA management software (Timedly)

### D. Real Estate Photography Platforms

#### Snappr (snappr.com)
- **What it does:** On-demand photography marketplace; accepts top 5% of photographers
- **Pricing:** Fixed prices from under $100; book with 2 hours' notice
- **Turnaround:** Edited photos delivered within 48 hours
- **Services:** Photography, virtual staging, photo editing, drone shots, videography

#### HomeJab (homejab.com)
- **What it does:** Assigns vetted real estate photographers near you
- **Turnaround:** Media delivered 24 hours after appointment

#### PhotoSesh (photosesh.com)
- **What it does:** On-demand/scheduled photography discount platform using GPS and calendar syncing
- **Scale:** 10,000+ photographers across North America and Europe

### E. Virtual Staging Platforms

#### BoxBrownie.com
- Full-service virtual staging and photo editing for real estate

#### VirtualStaging.com
- Virtual staging and photo editing outsourcing; $0.50-$4.80 per photo

#### Collov AI / Stager AI / AI HomeDesign
- AI-powered virtual staging; as low as $0.17 per image (Collov AI)
- Global virtual staging market: $0.35B in 2024, projected $5.98B by 2033 (26.4% CAGR)

### F. Presentation & Marketing Platforms

#### Highnote (highnote.io)
- **What it does:** AI-powered digital presentation platform for listing presentations, buyer presentations, offer submissions, and property marketing
- **Founded:** 2020 (by co-founder of Climb Real Estate and lead designer of DotLoop)
- **Pricing:** Free tier available; premium features with 14-day trial
- **Strength:** Tracking/analytics on when and how long materials are viewed

### G. Lead Qualification (Historical)

#### Agentology (now Verse.io - permanently closed)
- **What it was:** Lead qualification service combining AI-generated messages with human sales reps to vet and hand off leads
- **Funding:** $12M Series A in 2018; marketing agreement with Realogy
- **What happened:** Rebranded to Verse.io in 2019 to expand beyond real estate; original brand permanently closed
- **Lesson:** Pure lead qualification proved difficult to sustain as a standalone real estate product

### H. General Platforms Used for Real Estate Tasks

#### TaskRabbit
- Offers rental unit management and property showing assistance in some markets
- Some Taskers are licensed real estate agents
- Not purpose-built for real estate; limited real estate-specific features
- Taskers earn average $47/hour

#### Upwork / Fiverr
- General freelance marketplaces with real estate TC, marketing, and admin services
- Higher risk of ghosting, lack of industry knowledge, no real estate-specific vetting

### I. Open House Lead Capture Apps

## 2. Market Size & Trends

### Workforce Size
- **~3 million** licensed real estate agents in the United States
- **~1.49 million** NAR members (Realtors) as of 2025-26
- **~2.4 million** employed in the broader real estate, rental, and leasing sector
- **102,116** real estate brokerages in the U.S.
- **87%** of agents are independent contractors
- **55%** are affiliated with an independent (non-franchise) company

### Geographic Concentration
- Florida: 216,180 Realtors (most)
- California: 196,172
- Texas: 143,895
- Vermont: 1,713 (fewest)

### Agent Demographics
- **56.6%** women, **43.4%** men
- Average age: **49 years old**
- Median experience: **12 years** (up from 10 in prior year)
- 74% plan to remain active for at least 2 more years

### Income Data
- Median gross income: **$58,100** (2024, up from $55,800 in 2023)
- Agents with <2 years experience: median income of only **$10,100**
- Median sales volume per agent: **$2.5 million**

### Market Saturation
- **2.4 Realtors per active listing** nationally
- Headcount near record highs despite declining transaction volumes from pandemic peaks
- ~200,000 new NAR members added since start of pandemic

### NAR Settlement Impact (August 2024)
- Sellers no longer automatically responsible for buyer's agent commission
- Written buyer agreements now mandatory before touring homes
- Commission rates have barely changed in practice: buyer's agent commissions ~2.34-2.42%
- **63%** of agents report sellers still covering buyer-broker commissions
- **55%** report sellers offering competitive rates of 2.5%+
- Many agents considering leaving the industry due to commission uncertainty
- Long-term: may push agents to demonstrate clearer value and seek efficiency tools

### Overall Market
- Real estate agency/brokerage market: **$1.55 trillion** (2025), projected **$1.99 trillion** by 2029 (6.5% CAGR)
- Real estate tech market: expected to exceed **$41.52 billion** by 2025
- AI in real estate: grew from $222.65B to $303.06B (2024-2025), 36.1% CAGR
- Real estate transaction management software market: projected **$15.52 billion** by 2032

## 3. Common Tasks Agents Outsource

### High-Value In-Person Tasks (Require Licensed Agent or Local Presence)
1. **Showing assistance** - Covering buyer showings when the listing/buyer's agent is unavailable
2. **Open house hosting** - Sitting an open house on behalf of the listing agent
3. **Lockbox access / inspector & appraiser meet** - Being present to let inspectors, appraisers, or contractors into a property
4. **Property photography** - Professional listing photos, drone shots, video walkthroughs
5. **Physical staging** - Furnishing/decorating properties for sale
6. **Property condition reports** - Visiting properties and documenting their condition

### Administrative & Back-Office Tasks
7. **Transaction coordination** - Managing paperwork, deadlines, and compliance from contract to close
8. **MLS listing management** - Creating, updating, and maintaining MLS listings
9. **CRM management** - Data entry, lead tracking, pipeline management in tools like Follow Up Boss, kvCORE
10. **Email & calendar management** - Scheduling appointments, managing agent inbox
11. **Listing packet preparation** - Compiling property descriptions, photos, market analysis, disclosures

### Marketing Tasks
12. **Social media management** - Content creation, posting, engagement across platforms
13. **Email marketing** - Drip campaigns, newsletters, market updates
14. **Graphic design** - Listing flyers, mailers, social media graphics, digital ads
15. **Virtual staging** - AI or manual digital furniture placement in listing photos
16. **Photo/video editing** - Post-production on listing media
17. **Website management** - IDX site updates, blog content, landing pages

### Lead Generation & Communication
18. **Cold calling / prospecting** - Outbound calls to FSBOs, expired listings, sphere of influence
19. **Lead follow-up** - Responding to and nurturing inbound leads
20. **Client communication** - Appointment reminders, showing feedback collection, status updates

### Financial & Compliance
21. **Bookkeeping** - Commission tracking, expense management
22. **Market research / CMAs** - Comparative market analysis preparation
23. **Compliance auditing** - Ensuring documents meet brokerage and regulatory standards

## 5. Gap Analysis

### What Exists Today (and Its Limitations)

| Need | Current Solutions | Gap/Limitation |
|------|-------------------|----------------|
| Showing assistance | Showami | Only covers showings; limited to one task type |
| Transaction coordination | Transactly, individual TCs on Upwork | No marketplace model; hard to find/compare TCs |
| Photography | Snappr, HomeJab | Separate platforms; not integrated with agent workflow |
| Virtual staging | BoxBrownie, AI tools | Separate tools; no connection to agent task flow |
| Open house hosting | Showami (limited), informal networks | No dedicated marketplace; agents rely on brokerage colleagues |
| Marketing help | Upwork, Fiverr, VA companies | Not real estate-specific; quality/reliability issues |
| All-in-one freelance marketplace | Sticka (beta) | Too early; unproven; limited adoption |
| General task help | TaskRabbit | Not built for licensed real estate tasks |

### Key Gaps Identified

#### 1. No Unified "TaskRabbit for Real Estate"
The biggest gap is the absence of a single, trusted platform where agents can find help with ALL common tasks - showings, open houses, photography, transaction coordination, marketing, and more. Today, agents must use:
- Showami for showings
- Snappr/HomeJab for photography
- Upwork for TC/marketing help
- Informal networks for open house coverage
- VA companies for admin

**Opportunity:** A unified platform that aggregates all real estate-specific gig tasks in one place.

#### 2. No Peer-to-Peer Agent Task Exchange
Agents frequently need help from OTHER agents (not just vendors) - for showings, open houses, second opinions, mentoring, and licensed activities. The only significant platform addressing this is Showami, and it's limited to showings. There's no broad agent-to-agent help marketplace.

**Opportunity:** A platform enabling agent-to-agent task sharing across all licensed activities.

#### 3. New Agent Income Problem
Agents with <2 years experience earn a median of only $10,100/year. These agents need supplemental income while building their business. Meanwhile, busy experienced agents need help but can't find reliable licensed agents to assist.

**Opportunity:** Connect new agents (who need income) with experienced agents (who need help) - creating a two-sided marketplace with natural supply/demand dynamics.

#### 4. Post-NAR Settlement Value Demonstration
With mandatory written buyer agreements and increased commission scrutiny, agents need to clearly demonstrate their value. Platforms that help agents efficiently deliver services and track their contributions are increasingly important.

**Opportunity:** Tools that help agents log, track, and present the work they do for clients.

#### 5. Fragmented Vendor Discovery
Finding reliable local vendors (photographers, stagers, inspectors, contractors) is done through word-of-mouth and Google searches. There's no centralized, reviewed, real estate-specific vendor directory integrated with agent workflows.

**Opportunity:** A Yelp/Thumbtack-style discovery platform specifically for real estate service providers, with reviews from agents.

#### 6. No Integration with Existing Agent Tech Stack
Current gig/task platforms operate in isolation from agents' CRMs, transaction management tools, and MLS systems. This creates friction and manual data entry.

**Opportunity:** Deep integrations with Follow Up Boss, kvCORE, Dotloop, SkySlope, and MLS systems.

#### 7. Trust & Verification Gap
On general platforms (Upwork, TaskRabbit), there's no verification that freelancers hold active real estate licenses, carry E&O insurance, or understand local real estate regulations. This is critical for licensed activities.

**Opportunity:** Built-in license verification, E&O insurance checking, and real estate credential validation.

## 6. Monetization Models

### Models Used by Existing Platforms

#### 1. Transaction/Service Fee (Per-Task)
- **Showami model:** Initiating agent sets the rate ($45-$400); Showami takes a service fee from each showing (agent earns $28-$367, implying a ~17-18% platform fee)
- **Best for:** High-volume, low-cost tasks (showings, open houses)
- **Pros:** Low barrier to entry, pay-as-you-go, scales with usage
- **Cons:** Revenue is directly tied to transaction volume

#### 2. Subscription / SaaS Model
- **Used by:** CRMs (Follow Up Boss, kvCORE), transaction management (Dotloop, SkySlope), presentation tools (Highnote premium)
- **Pricing range:** $31.99/mo (Dotloop agent) to $499/mo (kvCORE)
- **Pros:** Predictable recurring revenue, high retention
- **Cons:** Higher barrier to adoption, requires demonstrating ongoing value

#### 3. Lead Generation / Pay-Per-Lead
- **Used by:** Zillow Premier Agent, Real Estate Bees, Realtor.com
- **Pros:** High willingness to pay (directly tied to revenue generation)
- **Cons:** Lead quality complaints common; competitive market

#### 4. Freemium
- **Used by:** Curb Hero (free, lender-sponsored), Highnote (free tier + premium), Glide (free agent tools)
- **Pros:** Rapid adoption, viral growth potential
- **Cons:** Conversion rates typically low (2-5%)

#### 5. Marketplace Commission
- **Used by:** Snappr, HomeJab (take percentage of photographer fees), Transactly (fee on TC services)
- **Standard range:** 15-25% of transaction value
- **Pros:** Aligns platform revenue with value delivered
- **Cons:** Pressure from both sides to reduce fees; risk of disintermediation

#### 6. Sponsored/Advertising Model
- **Used by:** Curb Hero (lender sponsorship), Zillow (agent advertising)
- **Pros:** Can offer free product to agents; lenders/title companies/vendors will pay for agent access
- **Cons:** Can feel intrusive; requires large user base to attract sponsors

#### 7. Virtual Assistant Retainer
- **Used by:** REVA Global, MyOutDesk, Virtudesk
- **Pricing:** $9.55-$10+/hour, typically billed monthly for set hours
- **Pros:** Predictable revenue, high LTV
- **Cons:** Higher commitment barrier, managing workforce

### Recommended Hybrid Model for a New Platform

A new real estate task/gig platform could combine:

1. **Free to join** for both task posters and task workers (maximize network effects)
2. **Service fee per task** (15-20% from the worker's payout, similar to Showami/Uber model)
3. **Premium subscription** for power users (priority listing, analytics, bulk posting, team features)
4. **Vendor/lender sponsorship** opportunities (sponsored placement, branded features)
5. **Integration partnerships** with CRMs and transaction management tools (revenue share or API access fees)

## 7. Key Takeaways & Opportunity Summary

### The Core Opportunity

There are **~1.5 million active Realtors** and **~3 million licensed agents** in the US. These agents:
- Spend **61% of their workweek** on administrative tasks
- Earn a median of only **$58,100/year** (and only $10,100 for new agents)
- Are **87% independent contractors** who must find their own support
- Operate across a **fragmented landscape** of 10+ different tools and platforms
- Face increasing **commission pressure** post-NAR settlement

### Why Now?

1. **NAR settlement** is forcing agents to justify their value and work more efficiently
2. **New agents flooding the market** (200K added since pandemic) need income while building their business
3. **Experienced agents** are drowning in tasks with no easy way to find reliable, licensed help
4. **Sticka** (the closest competitor to a unified marketplace) is in early beta, leaving the market wide open
5. **Showami** has proven the on-demand agent model works (80K agents, 97% pickup rate) but only for showings
6. **AI and automation** are changing agent workflows, creating new task types that need human support

### Competitive Advantages to Build

1. **License verification** - Verify active real estate licenses in real-time (differentiator vs. Upwork/TaskRabbit)
2. **Local matching** - GPS-based matching for in-person tasks (showings, open houses, photography)
3. **Broad task coverage** - Not just showings, but ALL agent tasks in one platform
4. **Two-sided value** - Help new agents earn income AND help busy agents find reliable help
5. **Integration-first** - Connect with the tools agents already use (Follow Up Boss, Dotloop, MLS)
6. **Trust system** - Agent-to-agent reviews, verified credentials, E&O insurance checking
7. **Mobile-first** - Agents live on their phones; the app must be fast and intuitive

### Estimated TAM (Total Addressable Market)

- 1.5M active agents x average potential spend of $200-500/month on outsourced tasks = **$3.6B - $9B annual TAM**
- Even capturing 1% = $36M - $90M annual revenue opportunity
- Comparable: Showami processes thousands of showings/month at $45-400 each with 80K agents
